Synoptic Picture

The near-blocked summer regime holds a fifth day but starts to loosen: the eastern severe engine winds down as a stratospheric PV streamer digs into Texas and Bertha closes on the Louisiana coast

The amplified, near-blocked summer pattern Tlaloc has tracked all week remains in command, but it is finally beginning to shift rather than simply repeat. The dominant 500 mb ridge is still centered over the central Plains and upper Midwest, with heights peaking near 5880-5940 dm, and it continues to anchor a formidable heat dome and a broad slack surface gradient across the interior West beneath a 1023-1025 mb high. What has changed is the eastern half: yesterday's anomalously cold Great Lakes cutoff has lifted northeast into a sharp closed low over New England (heights near 5500 dm), and the continent-spanning severe engine that delivered an Enhanced-risk day to the East Coast has relaxed accordingly. SPC today carries only a Slight risk over the southern Mid-Atlantic and Maine, and the Day 2 outlook shows the organized severe threat effectively shutting off Thursday even as the trailing cold front sags south — the ridge nudging west toward the Four Corners as a shortwave dives into the Missouri Valley marks the first real sign this blocked regime is starting to evolve.

Three centers of action define the map. The freshest is over the western Gulf and Texas: the 500 mb analysis shows a slow-moving, neutral-to-slightly-negative-tilt cutoff near the Louisiana-Texas coast, and Air Mass RGB makes the mechanism vivid — a cyclonically curved, deep red-orange stratospheric PV streamer sweeping into central Texas and wrapping a surface low near the TX-LA border, with brilliant overshooting tops over east Texas and western Louisiana. This is a genuinely new focal point, distinct in the mid-levels from Tropical Storm Bertha, whose surface center is drifting westward through the northern Gulf toward southeast Louisiana (note that the surface analyses disagree on Bertha's exact longitude; the NHC/WPC placement in the northern Gulf is the operative one). Bertha's outer bands threaten isolated to scattered coastal flash flooding along the I-10 corridor, though most of the heavy rain stays offshore and inland severe potential is limited by weakening and poor thermodynamics. The second and third focal points are the entrenched heat and the fading front: dangerous heat with 105-115 F heat indices and 75-80 F overnight lows persists across the Southeast and southern Plains, while the cold front from a deep Hudson Bay low continues to sweep damaging-wind and flash-flood potential from the Mid-South to the Northeast today.

Aloft, the story is a partially blocked flow that forces systems to the extremes — north into Canada or cut off to the south. A polar jet streak arcs across southern Canada and the Northeast, its sharp airmass boundary well depicted on the full-disk imagery, with a jet oriented from the mid-Atlantic into Atlantic Canada supporting the New England low and the East Coast frontal band. To the west a longwave trough with a slightly negative tilt digs into the Pacific Northwest and northern Rockies, keeping that corner cyclonic and cool. Canada carries as much of the upper-level story as the CONUS today: a 1033 mb high over the Prairies butts against the tightly wound Hudson Bay low, focusing a brisk, high-lift corridor from Manitoba through northwestern Ontario, and a 1039 mb high near Greenland is reinforcing high-latitude blocking that explains the sluggish progression of the eastern Canadian trough. That block, more than anything, is why the ridge-cool-trough couplet has been so slow to break down.

Climate Context

This is textbook peak-summer North America. The North American Monsoon is in full swing over the Desert Southwest and central/southern Rockies, mid-summer heat-and-severe season is driving both the entrenched Plains heat dome and the East Coast frontal severe threat, and the CPC 6-10 day outlook keeps a 90-100 percent above-normal signal over the southern Plains and Rockies against a cool trough over the Northeast — implying the ridge-dominated block persists well beyond the short range. Both hurricane basins are near their climatological ramp: the Atlantic is producing Tropical Storm Bertha in the northern Gulf but is otherwise quiet with no additional formation expected over seven days, while the busier East Pacific carries Hurricane Fausto west-southwest of Baja, a wave off Central America given a 90 percent chance of developing by the weekend, and a low southwest of Hawaii at 60 percent before it slides into the western Pacific.
