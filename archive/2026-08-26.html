Synoptic Picture

The late-summer dipole re-amplifies: a cold-core cutoff reforms over the Great Lakes and swings the severe stream toward the Northeast — but the ensembles cast it as a departing guest while the Southwest heat ridge reloads across the south

After yesterday's flattening toward progressive flow, the eastern half of the pattern has re-amplified. The 12Z 500 mb analysis shows a cold-core cutoff low over the upper Midwest and Great Lakes — closed 552-558 dam contours with a cold temperature core collocated with the height minimum — separated from the main westerlies and paired with the entrenched Desert Southwest/Great Basin ridge near 594 dam, a 35-40 dam couplet. The DuPage analysis reads this as blocked rather than progressive, and the geometry fits a self-retaining system that will pivot in place rather than translate quickly downstream. The important qualifier is that this is a transient re-amplification of the eastern trough, not the onset of a durable block: the ensemble pair (below) has the trough lifting northeast into Canada over the next one to two weeks while the ridge reloads and expands warmth across the south. Compared with yesterday's near-neutral, un-closed trough, today is a genuine step back toward amplitude on the eastern flank — but a temporary one.

Today's centers of action divide between the ridge and its periphery, the same split Tlaloc has tracked all week. The durable, dangerous element is the heat: WPC carries highs of 100-115 F across the Southwest and south-central U.S. with Major-to-Extreme HeatRisk over Texas and the Southwest through Thursday and poor overnight recovery — suppression and baking driven by midlevel subsidence and warming, a strengthened cap, and dry-air entrainment under the ridge, not by the modest 1017-1018 mb surface highs beneath it. The severe stream rings the cutoff: SPC's Day 1 Slight Risk runs from the southern Great Lakes and Midwest through the central/southern High Plains to the Ozarks, Mid-South, and Lower Mississippi Valley, with damaging winds the primary threat and large hail favored in the central High Plains upslope regime. The surface pattern supports this best where Gulf dewpoints in the mid-to-upper 70s meet the cold front draped from a 1006 mb northern-Rockies/Plains low across the central Plains and mid-South. The Day 2 outlook shows the threat pivoting downstream on Thursday: an embedded shortwave rotating around the Great Lakes trough carries a Slight Risk from Lake Erie into the Northeast (VT/NH, eastern New York), while a conditional big-hail/wind threat lingers over the central High Plains even as rising heights on the eastward-shifting ridge work to cap it. Elsewhere, a slow Southeast boundary prolongs heavy rain, and monsoon moisture keeps daily high-terrain convection going over the Southwest and Rockies. The other genuinely active surface feature is Pacific: a roughly 1000 mb coastal low off the Pacific Northwest with a cold front sweeping down the California/Nevada coast, where the tightest gradient on the map compresses into British Columbia.

The upper structure ties it together. The Air Mass RGB shows a broad, dry orange-red expanse over the central Plains, Texas, and northern Mexico that is ridge-top subsidence, not a stratospheric signature, while a compact reddish streamer curls cyclonically from the upper Midwest toward the Tennessee Valley on the cutoff's eastern flank; the abrupt color transition from Iowa-Illinois into the central Appalachians marks the implied jet corridor steering the convection. On the regime question, the ensemble pair is the test and it points the same direction as yesterday. The 6-10 day (Aug 31-Sep 4) has the ridge at 588 dam across the central and eastern CONUS, a closed low over the northwestern Canadian interior (about -90 m), and the zero-anomaly line along the U.S.-Canada border. The 8-14 day (Sep 2-8) retreats that trough northeast to the Arctic Archipelago/Baffin region while the ridge core settles over the Desert Southwest and northern Mexico (588 dam) with a broad +6 dam or greater positive anomaly across the central and southern CONUS. Read together, the positive anomaly holds and broadens across the south while the northern trough migrates from northwestern Canada to the Arctic — geographic contraction and an eastward/southward reload, not a collapse. Today's Great Lakes cutoff lifts out into that departing northern stream; the ridge does not vanish, it expands. The Day 2 note that the ridge shifts east toward the Plains is the first frame of that reload.

Climate Context

This is peak-summer North America close to the seasonal script: the North American Monsoon is active and feeding daily high-terrain convection across the Southwest and Rockies, and severe weather is expressed as ridge-periphery, northern-stream storms and frontal supercells rather than deep-Plains outbreaks. The ENSO backdrop is a decisively developing warm state — the ONI has climbed from weak La Niña in late 2025 through neutral in spring to roughly +1.4 C by late spring/early summer 2026, a clear trend toward at least a moderate El Niño. That configuration typically favors an active eastern Pacific while shear and dry air suppress the Atlantic, and even as the basin approaches its early-September climatological peak the tropics fit the split neatly: Tropical Storms Lala and Iselle plus a near-certain EP93 south of Mexico (80-90%), a developing broad low east-southeast of Hawaii, and two more candidate areas against only a low-end, deteriorating Atlantic wave (AL96, 60% but fading by the weekend). Summer teleconnection indices are weak, lagging cross-checks on the height field rather than drivers of it: a sharply negative NAO (-1.28) and a weakly negative, falling PNA are loosely consistent with sluggish Atlantic flow and slower eastward progression, but they should be read as no more than a nod to the story the height and PV fields already tell.
