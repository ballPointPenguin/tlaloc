Synoptic Picture

The summer block completes its westward rotation: heat ridge locks over the interior West and northern Rockies, Bertha is gone, and the day's action lives on the ridge's northern rim

That is a textbook omega-block configuration, and it is the single story organizing today's map. Tropical Storm Bertha, the headline feature of the last several days, has dissipated over south-central Texas and leaves only scattered showers. With the tropical wildcard gone, the pattern reduces to its skeleton: a strong ridge in the middle, a West Coast trough and an eastern downstream trough on the flanks, and the polar jet shunted well north into Canada. Systems are forced to the extremes, and the freshest energy rides the ridge's northern periphery.

The centers of action follow directly from that geometry. The primary severe focus is the Northern Plains — a Slight Risk across the Dakotas and northern Minnesota where multiple mid-level shortwaves and a surface trough overlap MLCAPE above 2000 J/kg and favorable deep-layer shear; large to very large hail is the main hazard, with a tornado threat if supercells persist as low-level shear increases into the evening. Overlaying everything is dangerous heat: 110s in the Desert Southwest, extreme-heat warnings across the western High Plains into Montana beneath the ridge core, and humid 90s from the southern Plains to Florida, while the North American Monsoon keeps firing daily storms with local flooding over the Southwest and Rockies.

Aloft, the block explains both the heat and its opposite. The western ridge anchors the large-scale flow and suppresses progressive shortwave motion, so the interior West bakes in stagnant anticyclonic flow; the jet and its sharp PV gradient arc from the northern Rockies through the Upper Midwest into the Great Lakes, vivid in Air Mass RGB as an orange-red stratospheric intrusion. That boundary is where the day's severe potential concentrates — deep tropical green air sits just south of it across the eastern two-thirds of the country, with convective cold tops already flaring over eastern Kansas-Missouri and the western Carolinas. Downstream, an amplified eastern trough keeps the Great Lakes and Northeast pleasant and seasonably cool behind an earlier cold front, the persistent cool corner of the couplet. SPC's Day 2 outlook shows the threat downshifting to Marginal on Saturday as the ridge expands eastward and caps the Upper Midwest, leaving a hot, well-mixed wind regime over South Dakota, a terrain-driven threat in the northern Rockies, and monsoon storms in southeast Arizona — the block loosens its grip on organized severe but does not break.

This is peak-summer North America running exactly to script. The North American Monsoon is in full swing over the Desert Southwest and central/southern Rockies, and mid-summer heat-and-severe season is driving both the entrenched interior heat dome and the marginal severe/flash-flood threats along the fronts; the CPC 6-10 day outlook reinforces this, with a 90-100 percent above-normal signal centered on the Great Basin and Four Corners and cool anomalies confined to the Great Lakes-to-Northeast trough, implying the western ridge persists and perhaps amplifies into early August.
