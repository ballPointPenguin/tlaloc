Synoptic Picture

The block's endgame is complete: a cutoff low over the upper Midwest anchors a central-and-eastern rain-and-severe corridor while the heat ridge settles into its Desert Southwest home

The multi-week summer block Tlaloc has tracked into its endgame has now finished its transition. Today's 500 mb analysis shows a robust western ridge firmly centered over the Desert Southwest and Great Basin (a 601 dam closed high near Nevada/southern California) opposite a deep, unseasonably amplified late-summer trough running from the northern Plains through the central Mississippi Valley, with a genuine cutoff low pinched off over the upper Midwest near the Illinois-Indiana line. This is an amplified ridge-trough dipole, not a Rex block: there is no closed cyclone stacked poleward of the ridge. The cutoff and the frontal systems draped beneath it are the day's engine. This is the third day of the ridge's southwestward retreat, and the severe corridor has marched east with it — from the Missouri/Illinois region two days ago to the Ohio and Tennessee Valleys today.

Today's busiest center of action is the eastern half of the country. A well-organized extratropical cyclone sits over the Ohio Valley-Great Lakes border on satellite, with its trailing cold front sweeping through the mid-Mississippi Valley and a separate Gulf Coast low (near 1008-1011 mb) parked over a deep moist-tropical airmass with upper-70s dewpoints from east Texas through the Southeast. SPC carries a Slight Risk across the Ohio and Tennessee Valleys, damaging wind the primary hazard under 35 kt shear, with an isolated tornado threat near the surface low and triple point over Indiana and Kentucky where destabilization allows. WPC overlays a broad Slight Risk for excessive rainfall from the Great Lakes through the Ohio and Tennessee Valleys into the Southeast, Gulf Coast, and southern High Plains, with localized flash flooding, and that threat translates eastward toward the Mid-Atlantic and New England by Monday. Canada carries real weight in the story: a strong 1038 hPa high over the Hudson Bay/Ungava region tightens the gradient across the western interior provinces, while the Day 2 picture brings an intense upper low from Alberta into Saskatchewan with a 50-70 kt 500 mb jet spreading into the northern High Plains — the leading edge of a genuinely progressive northern-tier flow.

Aloft the story is coherent. Air Mass RGB shows the cyclone's tight cyclonic curl and cold conveyor near the Ohio Valley, with a compact high-PV dry-intrusion tongue wrapping from the central Plains into the lower Mississippi and Tennessee corridors on the cyclonic, trough side of the flow. A sharp airmass boundary from the central Plains southwestward marks the implied jet corridor. Importantly, the broad red-orange over the central and southern Plains west of that tongue is warm, dry mid-to-upper tropospheric air under the downstream ridge — not a stratospheric feature — given its extent and its distance from the trough axis. The regime question is settled by the ensemble pair. The 6-10 day (Aug 5-9) keeps the western positive anomaly modest (roughly +3 to +6 dam) with its zero line back near the central Prairies and upper Midwest, and a deep cold low anchored over the Canadian Arctic Archipelago; the 8-14 day (Aug 7-13) re-consolidates the ridge but plants its core over the Desert Southwest and northern Mexico, leaving the northern Plains and Canadian Prairies in a below-normal trough regime on the cyclonic side of the Arctic low. Between the two leads the ridge contracts further southwest toward its climatological home while the north stays cool — a genuine geographic retreat, not a cosmetic flattening and not a collapse. The 6-10 day temperature outlook corroborates, anchoring the hottest 90-100% probabilities over the Great Basin, southern Rockies, and central/southern Plains while the northern tier and Great Lakes weaken. The teleconnection indices are a directional cross-check, not the basis: a rising PNA (+0.74) fits western ridging/eastern troughing, and a strongly positive AO (+1.23) fits the open, progressive polar flow and the deep-but-unblocked Arctic low — though summer signals are weak and secondary to the height field.

Climate Context

This is peak-summer North America, with heat-and-severe season driving both the entrenched interior warmth beneath the retreating ridge and the marginal severe threats ringing it, and the North American Monsoon in full swing over the Four Corners and New Mexico feeding the southern High Plains flash-flood and severe-wind threat. The ENSO backdrop remains one of steady cold-to-warm transition: after a weak La Niña through late 2025 (SON 2025 ONI -0.51), anomalies warmed through neutral in early 2026 and reached +0.98 by April-June, indicating a developing El Niño that will increasingly shape steering and shear into the back half of the season. The hurricane basins stay split against the climatological ramp toward the September peak — the Atlantic, Caribbean, and Gulf are quiet with no development expected over the next seven days, while the East Pacific holds Tropical Storm Genevieve west of the southern Baja California peninsula, with no additional formation anticipated.
