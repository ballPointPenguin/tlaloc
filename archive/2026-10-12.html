Synoptic Picture

Category 3 Simon stalls off Mexico as Great Basin low sets up Plains severe and record eastern warmth

Hurricane Simon is the main hazard today. At 12Z it was a Category 3 with 115 mph winds and a 958 mb pressure, down from Category 4 yesterday. It is nearly stationary about 50 miles west-southwest of Cabo Corrientes. Because it is crawling along the coast, rain is the bigger danger: isolated totals could reach 30 inches in Michoacan, Colima and Jalisco, with life-threatening floods and mudslides. In the US, a closed upper low dropping into the Great Basin brings heavy rain on the terrain from Utah to Arizona and snow in Nevada's ranges and the Wasatch. Excessive-rain risk peaks at Slight over eastern Utah and the Four Corners. There is no severe threat today: warm mid-level air caps the warm sector of a low over central Nebraska, leaving only elevated storms with small hail from the eastern Dakotas into the Upper Great Lakes. Plains and Midwest highs reach the 80s to low 90s, with daily records possible.

At 500 mb, an amplified ridge sits off the British Columbia coast. Flow plunges from it into a trough reaching from the northern Plains to the southern Rockies and northern Mexico, with the closed low embedded in it. Downstream, heights of 588-593 dam over the Southeast mark an unusually strong subtropical ridge for mid-October, which drives both the warmth and the capping. A 65-75 kt wind band runs from the northern Rockies to the Maritimes. This western trough is what NHC expects to eventually turn Simon northeast. The timing is uncertain, so steering stays weak and Simon lingers. Easterly shear and land interaction should weaken it despite 31-32 C water.

Tuesday, SPC has a Slight severe risk from the Texas and Oklahoma Panhandles to Kansas City. Instability (MLCAPE 1000-2000 J/kg) and 40-50 kt of shear favor supercells with large hail, later turning into damaging winds. A tornado or two is possible in New York. Wednesday could bring 2-3 inches of rain from western Missouri to the Big Bend. NHC expects Simon to make landfall around Thursday and dissipate by Saturday. Its leftover moisture streams north and keeps heavy rain going in the south-central US into Thursday. Record highs in the upper 80s to mid 90s continue in the Mid-South and Southeast through Thursday. After that, a trough digs into the East Friday and Saturday, bringing lake-enhanced rain, possibly snow, and a broad frost/freeze threat. This looks like a break rather than a regime change. CPC's 6-10 day mean shifts the main positive height anomaly to Greenland (about +21 dam), but the 8-14 day mean rebuilds positive anomalies over the central and eastern US.
