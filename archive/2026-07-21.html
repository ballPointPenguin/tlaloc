Synoptic Picture

The amplified, near-blocked summer pattern Tlaloc has tracked all week is still in command, but its eastern half has sharpened rather than faded. Three focal points define the map. First, severe weather: SPC carries an Enhanced risk from the Ohio Valley across the Mid-Atlantic into the Northeast today, with damaging wind the primary hazard and tornadoes and hail possible, as storms fire ahead of the eastward-moving cold front and pre-frontal trough in moderate-to-strong instability and effective shear. Second, the tropics: Tropical Storm Bertha, near 1004 mb in the Gulf, is drifting westward toward the north-central Gulf coast, with intense overshooting convection over the central Gulf and the Louisiana-Mississippi coast threatening coastal flash flooding along the I-10 corridor. Third, dangerous heat persists beneath the western ridge, with heat indices of 105-115 F expected across the Southeast and southern Plains and dangerous overnight lows.

Aloft, the polar jet is the connective tissue: a 95-115 kt jet streak arcs along the southern periphery of the trough from the central Rockies through the Ohio Valley into the Mid-Atlantic, placing favorable exit-region dynamics over precisely the severe corridor. Air Mass RGB makes the mechanism vivid — a robust orange-red stratospheric dry intrusion with elevated PV sweeps from the northern Plains and upper Midwest, its sharp southern gradient marking the jet and the leading edge of the descending PV streamer, while brilliant overshooting tops flag Bertha over the Gulf. The deep cold pool and closed contours mean this system is slow-moving, of a Rex/omega-block precursor character rather than fast zonal flow, which is why the blocked couplet — cool trough East, hot ridge interior West — is not breaking down. The CPC 6-10 day outlook reinforces that, holding a 90-100 percent above-normal heat signal over the central and southern Plains through late July against a persistent cool trough over the Northeast and Alaska.

Climate Context

This is textbook peak-summer North America, with one seasonal oddity worth flagging: the -45 to -54 C 500 mb cold pool over the upper Midwest is anomalously cold for mid-July and is what gives the eastern trough its bite. The North American Monsoon is in full swing over the Desert Southwest and central/southern Rockies, spilling into northwestern Mexico, while mid-summer heat-and-severe season drives both the East Coast severe corridor and the entrenched Plains heat dome. Both hurricane basins are near their climatological ramp: the Atlantic is producing Tropical Storm Bertha in the Gulf but is otherwise quiet with no additional formation expected over seven days, whereas the busier East Pacific carries Hurricane Fausto southwest of Baja plus a broad low south of Mexico given a 70 percent chance of development within a week. The ENSO backdrop is one of transition — guidance carries the state out of the weak La Niña that held through early 2026 toward warm-neutral and eventually El Niño conditions, a warm shift that will increasingly shape the back half of the Atlantic season.
