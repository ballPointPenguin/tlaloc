Synoptic Picture

Sheared Vance unravels toward Sinaloa while the warm East holds and a western trough loads

Tropical Storm Vance is the main hazard on the map, and it is falling apart. Three days ago it was a 95 kt hurricane. It is now at 40 kt (1002 mb), a figure NHC itself calls possibly generous, near 15.9N 106.9W, about 275 miles southwest of Manzanillo. It is moving northwest at 8 mph, with its thunderstorms sheared more than 100 nm southeast of an exposed low-level center. NHC turns it north toward Sinaloa and makes it a remnant low around 60 hours. The rain is the threat: 2-4 inches across central and southern Sinaloa, western Durango and coastal Nayarit and Jalisco through Thursday, locally near 7 inches in Sinaloa, with flooding and mudslides possible in steep terrain. The US is quiet. The only severe risk is a Marginal for a hail-producing storm or two in southeast New Mexico and far west Texas this evening. Snow is falling at higher elevations of the Cascades and northern Rockies.

Aloft, a closed low over Saskatchewan-Manitoba (551-555 dam, -21 C at 500 mb) extends a trough back to the Pacific Northwest. Brisk, nearly zonal flow crosses the northern tier, and a jet streak of 65 to over 80 kt is exiting southeastern Canada toward a mature cyclone with a dry slot east of the Maritimes. To the south, a flat 585-589 dam ridge with light winds covers the Gulf Coast and Southeast, and a 1035 mb surface high near Lake Michigan keeps the East dry and mild. The strong, increasing west-southwesterly shear wrecking Vance fits the west-southwesterly flow on the trough's southern flank, and dry midlevel air finishes the job. Meanwhile, easterly Gulf flow and leftover tropical moisture feed southern Texas, where WPC's discussion carries a Slight Risk of excessive rainfall (level 2 of 4) Wednesday night into Thursday.

Wednesday's shortwave into the Upper Mississippi Valley brings enough shear for organized storms, but too much inhibition for SPC to outline a severe area. From Friday, rain and storms over the southern Plains move into the Southeast for the weekend, with locally heavy totals. Out West, the Pacific Northwest low phases with southern-stream energy. That energy carries an unusually far-south (about 25N) surface low and an atmospheric river into southern California and northern Mexico. WPC's preferred guidance closes the trough off over the central US by Tuesday, November 3. Expect a deepening surface low, widespread rain, gusty winds and possible High Plains snow. The East stays 10-20 F above normal, with CPC's 6-10 day odds of a warm period at 70-80% from the Great Lakes to the Carolinas. That low should dent the warm regime rather than end it: the 8-14 day chart keeps positive height anomalies over the northern tier and eastern Canada while a deeper trough, near -15 dam, rebuilds over the Gulf of Alaska.
