Synoptic Picture

The block's eastern trough cuts off over the Mid-Atlantic — slow, soaking East, heat ridge rebuilding West

The summer block Tlaloc has tracked for roughly three weeks is still the whole story, and today it delivers exactly the eastern hand-off flagged yesterday. The 500 mb map shows a high-amplitude ridge centered over the central Plains (about 597 dm near eastern Colorado/Kansas), flanked by a longwave trough over the Pacific Northwest and a broad, neutral-to-slightly-negative-tilt trough over the eastern U.S. — a strongly amplified, blocked configuration. The key new development is that the eastern trough is going stationary: the SPC Day 2 outlook has it becoming a quasi-stationary upper low over the Mid-Atlantic with troughing draped south down the Atlantic coast. That transition from a progressive negative-tilt trough to a cutoff is what matters most, because it locks the wet, unsettled regime over the East for days while the heat ridge re-anchors and expands westward — the CPC 6-10 day outlook shows a 90-100 percent above-normal bullseye over the Desert Southwest, Great Basin, and California with no hint of breakdown into early August.

Today's centers of action follow the block's rim. The eastern trough is the busiest: WPC carries a moderate risk of excessive rainfall and flash flooding across the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast along a slow-moving frontal zone, with SPC slight-risk severe (damaging winds, hail, isolated tornadoes) from the Mid-Atlantic into the Carolinas as northwesterly flow of 45-55 kt overspreads a moist prefrontal airmass. Air Mass RGB confirms deep convection with cold tops over the central Appalachians into the mid-Atlantic and a second cluster near the Mississippi-Alabama border. Canada carries real weight again: a chain of lows and fronts trails from a 999 hPa low over the northern Plains up through Saskatchewan and Manitoba, where the tightest isobar spacing marks the strongest surface winds and a lee-cyclone corridor, while a slow occluded/stationary front and a Great Lakes low keep Ontario and Quebec active and a string of weak lows near Newfoundland and Labrador keeps Atlantic Canada unsettled. Under the ridge, dangerous heat holds across the southern tier (heat indices 105-115) and the North American Monsoon fires afternoon storms across the Four Corners, Rockies, and High Plains. Looking to Wednesday, the severe focus migrates south with the cutoff to southern Georgia and northern Florida (wet microburst threat in 3000-4000 J/kg buoyancy) plus a deepening lee cyclone over the northern High Plains that SPC flags as a possible upgrade candidate.

Aloft, the block explains both extremes cleanly. A vivid stratospheric dry intrusion — high-PV air descending on the anticyclonic side of the ridge — arcs as a red-orange swath from the central High Plains through the Texas Panhandle into northern Mexico, with the jet axis running west-northwest to east-southeast from the southern Rockies through the Ohio Valley, sharply separating that dry airmass from the deep tropical green plume over the Gulf and Southeast. A coherent PV streamer draped across the Upper Midwest and Great Lakes is curling cyclonically toward a developing baroclinic system over the northeastern U.S. and western Atlantic — the surface manifestation of the trough winding up before it cuts off. With two ridges flanking a stalling trough, storm motions stay slow and the flash-flood threat compounds over the East, while the entrenched western ridge keeps the interior baking.
