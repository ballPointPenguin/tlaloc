Looking ahead, WPC's extended guidance keeps the large-scale wave train intact: shortwave energy digging into the Midwest/Great Lakes and a building northeastern trough gradually suppress the ridge's northeast side, easing the northern-Plains heat early next week while shifting and consolidating dangerous heat south into the central Plains, Mississippi Valley, and Gulf Coast. Climate Context

This is textbook peak-summer North America: the North American Monsoon is in full swing over the Southwest and northwestern Mexico, and mid-summer heat-and-severe season is driving the heat domes, terrain convection, and Plains frontal storms. The CPC 6-10 day outlook confirms the amplified regime is expected to persist — a high-confidence, 90-100% above-normal signal over Texas and the Gulf Coast with a western ridge axis, opposite a well-defined below-normal Great Lakes/Ohio Valley trough — matching the retrograding, ridge-west/trough-east evolution now underway. Tropically, the East Pacific remains the busy basin, with Tropical Storm Elida under advisory and a wave south-southwest of Mexico given a high (90% over 7 days) chance of developing this weekend, while the Atlantic stays quieter apart from the low-odds northeastern Gulf feature. The ENSO backdrop is a transition: the weak La Niña that held through early 2026 (ONI around -0.5) has given way to warming, with AMJ 2026 near +0.98 and guidance trending toward El Niño development — a warm phase that will increasingly shape the second half of hurricane season.
