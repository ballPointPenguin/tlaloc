Synoptic Picture

Category 4 Simon hugs Mexico's coast as Rachel's remnants flood the Southwest, Isaias threatens Carolina tornadoes

Yesterday's three tropical cyclones are now ashore or close to it, and Simon is the most dangerous. NHC has it at Category 4, 130 kt and 933 mb (928 mb found overnight), 60 miles west of Manzanillo and creeping north-northwest at 6 mph. The core hugs the coast through Monday, and NHC still can't say whether or when it makes landfall. Expect catastrophic wind and surge from Punta San Telmo to El Roblito and 10-20 inches of rain, up to 30 in Michoacan, Colima and Jalisco. Rachel's fast-moving remnant dissipates today, but its moisture drives a WPC Moderate flood risk from Southern California to southern Utah, with over 3 inches on the Peninsular Ranges and southern Wasatch. Isaias's remnant low near Nashville brings 1-3 inches (locally 5) to the central Appalachians and Mid-Atlantic and a Slight severe risk in North Carolina and Virginia, mainly 18-00Z. Elsewhere, near-record 80s-90s heat covers the Plains, and Montana gets its first winter storm, with more than a foot of snow.

At 500 mb, a deep, positively tilted trough runs down the northern Rockies. A 550 dam closed-looking center sits off the Pacific Northwest, and an 80 kt jet streak rounds the trough base. Downstream, a broad 580-588 dam ridge covers the Plains to the Southeast. The trough and ridge sit side by side, not stacked, so this is no Rex block. The strong southwesterly flow between them pushes Rachel's moisture toward Montana and pumps warm air into the heat. Isaias rides the ridge's western flank into the Atlantic Monday. SPC expects no severe weather Monday.

The upper low lingers over the Great Basin into midweek and draws Simon's leftover moisture north. That brings heavy rain to the southern-central Plains and mid-Mississippi Valley Wednesday-Thursday, while record heat holds from Texas to the Southeast. WPC, with low confidence, then carves a deep central-eastern trough Friday-Saturday; the ECMWF and UKMET hold the low west instead. CPC's 6-10 day heights turn zonal, but the 8-14 day chart rebuilds +3 to +9 dam anomalies over the Great Lakes and Mississippi Valley. The eastern trough may be only a temporary interruption, and the data can't settle that yet. An eastern Pacific depression is likely south of Mexico by midweek or later (80%).
