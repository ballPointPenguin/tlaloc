Synoptic Picture

Occluding Midwest low sweeps cold front east; marginal Mid-South severe, Southeast Florida flooding persists

The Plains cyclone behind the last two nights of severe storms is now an occluding Midwest system, and today's threat is weaker than yesterday's. At 09Z a low of about 1000 mb sat near the Kansas-Missouri border, with its cold front running through eastern Oklahoma and Texas into the western Gulf. SPC has only a Marginal risk for the mid and lower Mississippi Valley and East Texas: broken lines with isolated damaging gusts and perhaps a brief tornado, most likely in eastern Arkansas and northern Louisiana. Shear there is 40-50 kt but instability is only 500-1000 J/kg. Farther north, strong low-level shear over Missouri, Iowa and Illinois is largely wasted because lapse rates are poor. WPC's Slight flash-flood risk follows the Mississippi corridor from eastern Arkansas into western Kentucky, while steady rain covers the northern Plains and Upper Midwest. Separately, onshore easterlies keep a Slight flood risk around Miami-Fort Lauderdale, where Flood Watches stay up after damaging flooding yesterday.

Aloft, the ejected western trough has closed off at 500 mb over Iowa, Illinois and Wisconsin. A 55-65 kt jet streak runs along its eastern flank toward the Ohio Valley. Satellite shows a dry slot wrapping the low's south side over Kansas and Oklahoma, the mark of a mature occlusion: the strongest lift is moving north, away from the Gulf moisture. Behind it, an amplified ridge over the northern Rockies and western Canada steers a cold 1030 mb high south with strong northerlies, while the East stays mild under 580-590 dam heights. On Wednesday the low moves into Upper Michigan, and SPC expects no severe storms as scattered storms run from Kentucky to Georgia.

The front reaches the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic on Thursday and may stall Friday with localized flash flooding. A Four Corners shortwave brings Central Rockies snow Wednesday and Thursday. After that, a pattern sets in with a trough over the northeast Pacific, a ridge over the West and central US, and a trough in the East. That means repeated Pacific rain for Washington, Oregon and the Cascades, and weekend storms for the southern Plains and western Gulf Coast. A shortwave diving into the East could close off a low over the Mid-South, so rainfall there is uncertain. CPC's ridge over western Canada and the northern Plains rebuilds to about +9 dam for Nov 10-16 rather than drifting toward the Southwest. That makes the ridge a lasting feature: the High Plains go from cold this week to 80-90% odds of above-normal temperatures.
