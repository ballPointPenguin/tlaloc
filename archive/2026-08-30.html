Synoptic Picture

The week's blocked dipole relaxes: the Great Basin cutoff washes out, leaving a sprawling south-central heat ridge with progressive northern-stream storms on its flank and a Pacific storm closing on British Columbia

The amplified, blocked dipole that dominated Tlaloc all week has relaxed into a more progressive configuration. Yesterday's Great Basin cutoff is gone; the 500 mb analysis now shows a broad, strong late-summer ridge cresting near 594 dam over the south-central Plains (central Oklahoma/Kansas), faced downstream by an amplified but positive-tilt — and therefore progressive and weakening — trough over the Great Lakes and upper Midwest, plus a separate closed low off the Southeast Atlantic coast. This remains an amplified longwave pattern, but it is no longer a stalled block: the western pole has washed out and the flow along the ridge's northern flank has gone fast. At the surface the map is benign for late summer — shallow heat/lee lows (1003-1008 mb) over the High Plains and southern Rockies, a cold front draped from the northern Great Lakes westward into Montana, and a sprawling 1030 mb Atlantic high ridging into the Southeast. Over Canada a strong 1034 hPa high anchors the Prairies/northern Plains, while a Pacific frontal low near 1000 hPa descending from the Gulf of Alaska closes on the British Columbia coast, where the isobars bunch into the tightest gradient on either analysis.

Today's centers of action ride the ridge's northern flank. SPC carries a Slight Risk over the northern Plains: an upper trough ejecting out of Idaho reaches eastern Montana and the western Dakotas by late afternoon, where near-90F surface temperatures ahead of the cold front fuel storms expected to congeal into a damaging-wind line racing into the Dakotas overnight — an active mesoscale discussion is already tracking a supercell east of Williston. Secondary threats set up over the central Colorado mountains into western Nebraska/Kansas and again across Utah into western Colorado. The North American Monsoon keeps a WPC Slight Risk for excessive rainfall over the Southwest, Great Basin, and Central Rockies through Monday. Along the decaying front from North Carolina to the lower Mississippi Valley, storms shift to the Gulf Coast through Tuesday. In the tropics, a low south of Louisiana carries a 30 percent seven-day development odds while Dolly's remnants soak Puerto Rico and the Leewards.

The Air Mass RGB draws the mechanism cleanly: the broad dry orange over the south-central and southeastern U.S. is warm, subsiding, low-PV air under the ridge — not a stratospheric intrusion — and that mid-level subsidence, warming, and capping (not the modest surface highs) is what builds and holds the heat; a sharper orange ribbon along the southern Rockies into northern Mexico and the western Gulf marks the implied jet corridor on the ridge's southwest flank, while the full disk shows a cooler blue-green airmass poleward of the Great Lakes — the frontal/jet boundary feeding the northern-tier storms. On the regime question, the ensemble pair says the high-latitude block persists but the south-central anchor is on the move. The 6-10 day chart (Sep 4-8) throws its dominant positive anomaly far north over the Canadian Arctic Archipelago and Baffin Island (about +90 m), keeps only a weak +30-40 m over the south-central U.S., suppresses any western ridge behind a northeastern-Pacific negative anomaly, and trends CONUS mid-latitudes toward more zonal flow. The 8-14 day chart (Sep 5-11) then loses the south-central positive entirely and relocates the positive core northwest to western Canada, the northern Rockies, and the northern Plains, while the Arctic ridge and the northeastern-Pacific trough persist. Read across the pair, the positive core migrates north and northwest rather than reloading over the south-central U.S. — a real departure from the past several days' "southwestward contraction and reload" read. WPC's days 3-7 tempers the timing, keeping the ridge entrenched with the heat core over the central/southern Plains and Mid/Lower Mississippi Valley into the second week of September and only the eastern side troughing behind a next-weekend front; so near term the heat holds, but the longer lead argues the south-central heat regime is genuinely relaxing and relocating rather than regenerating in place. The durable element now is the Arctic/Canadian block, loosely consistent with the strongly negative NAO (7-day mean -0.90, now -1.28).

Climate Context

This is peak-summer North America near the seasonal script, with the Atlantic basin approaching its early-September climatological peak. The North American Monsoon remains active, feeding daily high-terrain convection across the Southwest and Rockies, and severe weather continues to express itself as ridge-periphery, northern-stream and frontal storms rather than deep-Plains outbreaks. The ENSO backdrop is a developing warm state: the ONI has climbed from weak La Niña in late 2025 through neutral early in 2026 to roughly +1.39C by June-July 2026, i.e. a developing moderate El Niño. That configuration typically favors an active eastern Pacific while shear and dry air suppress the Atlantic, and the current tropics fit that split — Hurricane Karina and Tropical Storm Lowell are under advisory alongside a 90 percent development area south of Mexico and a 30 percent area near Hawaii, against a quieter Atlantic where Dolly's remnants and a homegrown Gulf low each carry only modest odds (though the Gulf remains capable of spinning up its own systems regardless of basin-wide shear). Teleconnection indices lag the height field and are used here only as a cross-check: the strongly negative NAO is directionally supportive of the high-latitude Canadian Arctic blocking the ensembles keep, and the deepening negative PNA is loosely consistent with the suppressed western ridge and the eastern troughing building late in the period.
