Synoptic Picture

A transient 594-dam ridge crowns the northern Plains today, but the durable signal is an Arctic trough digging over Canada that shoves the heat regime south into the Deep South and Southwest

The organizing story is a high-amplitude pattern in mid-reorganization. This morning's 500 mb analysis puts the ridge's warm core over the northern Plains and upper Midwest, roughly 594 dam with the chart's warmest midlevel air (near -8 to -10 C) beneath it, and the Great Basin still holds heights near 596-597 dam — so broad ridging still dominates the western and central US, and WPC keeps dangerous, record-threatening heat over the West this weekend. But this northern-tier ridge is the transient piece, not the regime. At the surface a broad high pressure field spans the central and eastern CONUS (a 1025 mb center over the Southeast, a 1031 mb reading over the central High Plains that deserves some caution), while over Canada a 1033 hPa anticyclone anchors Hudson Bay and Prairie cyclogenesis — two systems flagged NEW near the Alberta-Saskatchewan border — trails a cold front into the north-central US. That front, plus a stationary boundary over the upper Midwest and a cold front draped down the central Plains into Texas, rings the ridge and locates today's lift.

Today's centers of action line up along the airmass boundary and the fronts. SPC carries a Slight Risk for damaging winds along the I-95 corridor from D.C. to New York, where strong heating in a moist airmass fires afternoon clusters beneath the passing upper disturbance. The heavy-rain focus is twofold: an organizing convective cluster over the northern Gulf near the Louisiana-Mississippi coast, growing on the western edge of the moist airmass where it meets drier air, and monsoon storms over Arizona and western Mexico with localized flash-flood potential.

Aloft the pieces fit. Genuine stratospheric-intrusion signatures — bounded, cyclonically curling dry slots — are confined to cutoff lows out over the north Atlantic, not our continent. The regime question is the interesting one, and the ensemble pair settles it in favor of transition-by-contraction rather than rebuild-in-place. The 6-10 day (Aug 12-16) flattens the CONUS toward zonal flow with near-zero to weakly negative anomalies, parking the only real positive anomaly offshore over the northeast Pacific and Gulf of Alaska where it does not penetrate inland. The 8-14 day (Aug 14-20) then rebuilds a ridge — but displaced south, its core over the Four Corners and southern Plains, with a deep Arctic trough dug over the Canadian Archipelago and the northern Rockies and northern Plains left near-normal to slightly below. The ridge is retreating toward the Southwest and southern tier while the north cools; it is not regenerating over the northern Plains where it sits today. WPC's extended discussion says the same in plain language: two troughs (northeast Pacific and Hudson Bay), a Southwest ridge that weakens and shifts east into the Southern/Central Plains and Deep South, and cooling arriving over the northern Plains, upper Midwest, and Great Lakes. This extends the transition Tlaloc flagged yesterday, though it refines the endpoint: rather than the ridge core detaching cleanly to Alaska, the durable ridging contracts south onto the southern US tier, with the offshore Pacific positive anomaly the only high-latitude survivor.

The ENSO backdrop is a developing warm state: the provided ONI table climbs from weak La Niña in late 2025 (near -0.61) through neutral in late winter and into moderate El Niño by mid-2026, reaching +1.39 for the summer season, placing early August under a warm-ENSO influence. The daily teleconnection indices (a weakening, slightly negative PNA and an NAO plunge to strongly negative) are directional summer signals only and lag the height field, but they cross-check the story the analysis and ensembles already tell: a western/northern ridge losing its grip and a volatile, trough-dominated high-latitude circulation.
