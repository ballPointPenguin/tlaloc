Synoptic Picture

Southeast coastal low lifts north as western trough swings east; bigger East Coast storm eyed for Thanksgiving

The weekend's main hazard is a large non-tropical low a few hundred miles east of northeastern Florida, analyzed near 1013 mb at 15Z. WPC moves it slowly north to the northern Mid-Atlantic coast by Sunday evening. NHC gives it only a 10 percent chance of tropical or subtropical development, and falling, but it will still drive gales offshore, dangerous surf, rip currents and coastal flooding from the Southeast to the Mid-Atlantic into early next week. Today's Slight risk of excessive rainfall hugs the coast from northeast Florida to South Carolina. On Sunday a Marginal risk moves to the New Jersey-to-southern New England shoreline. The severe threat is low. SPC has a Marginal risk over south Florida, where a weak low and a roughly 30 kt low-level jet could support a tornado or two, and another over northwest Oklahoma into southern Kansas, where storms on a cold front sagging south could produce hail and gusts.

Aloft, an amplified but progressive trough covers the West and central US. It is tilted positive to neutral, with its base over the Desert Southwest and northern Mexico. Two small closed lows, one near the Pacific Northwest and one over the northern Rockies, are still embedded in the westerlies rather than cut off. Heights drop from 578-580 dam on the Gulf Coast to the mid-540s over the northern Plains. A 65-85+ kt jet runs from the southern Rockies to the Great Lakes, with its core near Iowa and Wisconsin. Compact high-PV streaks on satellite mark shortwave energy diving into the trough. The Southeast sits in weak 10-30 kt flow under a broad ridge, which is why the coastal low drifts instead of racing out to sea. At the surface, the Plains front reaches the Lower Great Lakes and western Gulf Coast by Sunday evening, bringing rain and then light snow to the Upper Midwest. A Pacific front brings rain and mountain snow to the Northwest, northern California and, on Sunday, the Sierra.

Next week WPC expects a frontal wave over Florida to grow Wednesday into a deepening cyclone that tracks up the East Coast through Thanksgiving. It would bring heavy rain, coastal wind damage and flooding, possible wintry precipitation in the Appalachians where cold air is trapped east of the mountains, and colder wraparound air for interior New England. How deep the central US trough gets is the main uncertainty. An eastern Pacific low brings heavy rain and wind to northern California and the Northwest Tuesday and Wednesday. WPC builds a western ridge late in the week, but CPC's 6-10 and 8-14 day outlooks keep the strongest positive height anomaly over the Arctic, with no positive anomaly over the northern Rockies, so a western ridge would likely be short-lived. Odds favor warmth across the northern tier, highest at 70-80 percent in the Dakotas.

Climate Context

A strong El Niño frames the pattern. The Oceanic Niño Index reached +2.46 for August-October, and it is still rising, though more slowly than before (about +0.3 per season, down from +0.5). That fits the setup: an active southern stream feeding Gulf and Southeast coastal lows, a cool lean along the Gulf Coast, and mild Pacific air over the northern tier in CPC's outlooks. Atlantic hurricane season ends November 30. Today's offshore low is non-tropical, and NHC expects no development in the eastern Pacific. The teleconnection indices are three weeks old, so they are not used here.
