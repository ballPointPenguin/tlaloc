Synoptic Picture

Great Lakes trough and southern-stream wave set up powerful Thanksgiving coastal storm for the East

No severe weather is expected today or Tuesday. The story is the Thanksgiving coastal storm flagged in recent notes, which WPC now calls high-confidence. A deep Great Lakes and Ontario/Quebec trough brings advisory-level snow to Michigan today and to western New York and northwestern Pennsylvania tonight. Meanwhile the Gulf of Maine low (about 996 mb at 09Z) lifts into southeastern Canada. A southern-stream shortwave near the Four Corners spreads mountain snow over the central Rockies today and the southern Rockies and Texas Panhandle Tuesday. Between them, a 1032-1034 mb high holds cold, dry air over the northern Plains. Out west, a very deep Gulf of Alaska low (near 966 hPa at 00Z) drives fronts into B.C. and the Northwest. That brings a Marginal flood risk on the Olympic coast and High Wind Advisories in Oregon.

At 500 mb the eastern trough bottoms near 529 dam north of Lakes Huron and Superior and may be closed. A jet core above 70 kt arcs from the northern Plains through the Great Lakes into southeastern Canada. The West has split flow, with a shallower northern Rockies trough and a modest southern-stream jet across Texas and the Gulf. SPC expects that High Plains trough to sharpen as it digs across the Rio Grande Tuesday, forming a northern Gulf low late Tuesday into Wednesday. Strong offshore flow keeps the moist low-level air out over the Gulf, so Gulf Coast storms stay elevated and weak. The key step is phasing. WPC merges the two streams over the Midwest Wednesday night into Thursday, letting the low tap moisture 3-5 standard deviations above normal and a strong plume of water vapor aimed at the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast (750-1000 kg/m/s).

Rain reaches the Gulf Coast Tuesday evening and the Southeast Tuesday night, with a Slight Risk of flash flooding on the Carolina coast Wednesday. Thanksgiving Day is the peak. Expect 1-4 inches of rain in the Carolinas and Mid-Atlantic and 1-2 in the Northeast. Gusts top 50 mph on the coast and 40 mph inland along I-95, and significant coastal flooding is possible from Georgia to New England. Winds ease Friday night as the low reaches the Maritimes. Snow showers behind it look minor, a smaller wintry role than earlier expected. Highs run below average in the south-central U.S. Thursday and Friday, with frost possible in the southern Plains. The northern Cascades get heavy snow. Further out, CPC's ensemble means show heights 21 dam above normal over Greenland and the Arctic islands and a Gulf of Alaska trough 9 dam below normal. Flow over the lower 48 is near-zonal, and both features persist at 8-14 days. CPC favors warmth nationwide Nov 28-Dec 2, with no cold signal despite the Arctic ridge.
