Synoptic Picture

The heat dome's breakdown slips to slow motion: the southern ridge buys more record days as a fresh Pacific Northwest cutoff reloads the western trough

The clean regime breakdown Tlaloc flagged yesterday is not so much cancelled as slowed and reshaped. Rather than the southern ridge simply eroding, today's map has settled into a durable two-wave configuration: a fresh closed low has cut off over the Pacific Northwest coast near the Oregon-Washington latitude, reloading the western trough, while a robust 500 mb ridge (588-594 dam) arches over the northern tier, Great Lakes, and Ontario. Anchoring the north end of the pattern is a deep Arctic/Hudson Bay trough with a cluster of 976-998 mb lows over the Shield, feeding a cool, cyclonic regime and a frontal zone across Ontario and Quebec. So the story is a heat dome that is contracting and reconfiguring, not collapsing.

The day's sharpest action sits on the ridge's northern and western periphery. A slow-moving front across the north-central Plains and Missouri Valley is the primary rain engine: WPC has a Moderate Risk for excessive rainfall over northeast Kansas and northwest Missouri this morning, with the flash-flood focus shifting north into the Northern Plains, Upper Midwest, and Ohio Valley through Friday, then spreading into the Great Lakes and Northeast this weekend before dropping toward the Mid-Atlantic early next week. A second focus is the reinvigorated Southwest monsoon, with southern Arizona and New Mexico upgraded to a Moderate Risk for 2-3 inches. Along the heat dome's southern edge a stationary front keeps daily downpours going over Florida. Severe potential is limited and ridge-peripheral: SPC carries only a Marginal Risk across the Midwest/Ohio Valley, where a mid-level vorticity max over Illinois/Indiana tracks east into poor lapse rates, and a separate Marginal over Utah and northwest Colorado where strong shear meets weak Great Basin instability.

The upper-level structure ties this together. The Air Mass RGB draws a sharp boundary from the central Plains southwestward through Texas into northern Mexico, marking the jet corridor along the frontal wave, while a brighter dry streamer wrapping from the northern Rockies southeast across Kansas and Missouri is the credible jet-related intrusion at the base of the downstream trough — the piece driving the heavy-rain forcing. The broad red-orange drying over the Desert Southwest, Great Basin, and northern Rockies is warm-season ridge-top subsidence given its sheer extent, not a stratospheric signature. On the regime question, the two ensemble leads point the same way: the western ridge that stands along the West Coast into the Southwest in the 6-10 day (576-582 dam) retreats northwest toward the eastern Pacific and Alaska by the 8-14 day, and over the CONUS the positive height anomalies contract to the Gulf Coast and Southeast, while the Arctic-to-central-Canada trough (roughly 6-9 dam below normal, a polar vortex displaced south toward central Canada) is the anchor that holds. That is ridge contraction and eastward reconfiguration with the southern warmth fading over the south-central U.S. and consolidating toward the Southeast — not a ridge regenerating in place, but also not the clean collapse. The honest read remains a cooling of the north with the heat retreating southeast, in a moderately amplified, still fairly progressive regime.
