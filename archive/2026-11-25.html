Synoptic Picture

Thanksgiving coastal storm deepens off the Carolinas as a central US upper low swings east

The Thanksgiving coastal storm we've been tracking for days is now organizing. Low pressure on the front off the Southeast coast will deepen today, come ashore in North Carolina around 09-12Z Thursday, reach Virginia by evening and then head up the coast. The main threats are coastal flooding, high surf and beach erosion from Georgia to the Northeast. Gusts could top 50 mph on the coast and 45 mph inland to I-95 and the central Appalachians. High Wind Warnings cover New Jersey, Delmarva and eastern Pennsylvania. The winds come from the tight squeeze between the deepening low and a 1036-1040 mb high off New England. Rain totals of 1-4 inches are expected in the Carolinas and Mid-Atlantic, with 1-2 inches in southern New England. There is a Slight Risk of excessive rainfall on the North Carolina coast today, shifting to the New Jersey coast and Long Island Thursday. Severe risk is limited. South Florida and the Upper Keys have a Slight Risk today, but the air is only weakly unstable (about 100 J/kg at Key West), so a tornado is possible only if surface-based storms form. The Carolina barrier islands could see an isolated gust or tornado early Thursday, then only elevated thunderstorms. Winter Storm Warnings cover snow in the Middle Mississippi Valley, and Texas has freeze warnings.

At 500 mb, a closed or nearly closed low sits near the Iowa-Nebraska border, with heights in the low 550s dam and temperatures of -20 to -30 C. Satellite shows a small, dry comma-shaped patch right over the low, which fits dry stratospheric air being pulled down. A 55-70 kt jet wraps around the base of the trough and turns northeast over the Gulf Coast and southern Appalachians. Those southwesterly winds spread out aloft over the coastal low, helping it deepen. On Thursday, SPC expects the upper low to swing northeast across the Tennessee Valley while a lead wave lifts north over the Mid-Atlantic. A 570-580 dam ridge over the Northeast slows the whole system down.

By Saturday the upper trough is over New England and the storm moves into the Maritimes. Wind and coastal flooding continue until the gradient eases Sunday. A Northern Rockies shortwave then forms a Mississippi Valley low Sunday, with showers and storms in the Lower Mississippi Valley and Southeast. Pacific systems reach the West Coast from Sunday on. The deeper one should bring heavy rain to coastal California and heavy Sierra snow around Tuesday, though its timing is uncertain. CPC's 6-10 and 8-14 day height outlooks replace today's slow, wavy pattern with faster west-to-east Pacific flow: troughs in the Gulf of Alaska and the Southwest, ridging over eastern Canada. Above-normal temperatures are favored across nearly the whole Lower 48, with 70-80 percent odds in the central corridor.
