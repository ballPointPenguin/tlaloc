Synoptic Picture

Nor'easter pounds the Mid-Atlantic coast while a digging West Coast low pulls tropical moisture into the Southwest

The nor'easter off the Carolinas, now in its fourth day, is still the main story. NHC tracks it as AL93, a storm-force non-tropical low a few hundred miles offshore that is still attached to an occluded front. WPC expects it to strengthen and drift slowly north. Onshore winds will drive coastal flooding at high tide, dangerous surf and beach erosion from southern New Jersey to North Carolina. Gusts will top 40 mph near the coast, strongest on the Outer Banks. NHC gives it a 40 percent chance of briefly turning subtropical. In the West, a closed low digging south off California brings only scattered Southwest storms today and a Marginal flash-flood risk in southeast Arizona. There is no severe threat anywhere. Off southern Mexico, EP94 (90 percent) should become a depression within a day and stay offshore.

Aloft, the flow over the lower 48 is mostly zonal. A 588-590 dam ridge sits over the Gulf, and the strongest flow, 30-50 kt, runs along the northern tier. North of Montana and the Dakotas sits an open trough with an unseasonably cold -40 to -45 C pool at 500 mb. Its surface reflection is a modest frontal wave over Minnesota, with a cold front trailing toward Texas. A 1035 mb high north of the Great Lakes keeps the East cool and dry and helps drive the onshore wind. The western low matters most: a strong southwesterly jet on its east side will draw very moist air north, with the best lift arriving late tonight.

On Wednesday and Thursday, WPC has Slight flash-flood risks over central and northern Arizona into the Four Corners and on the Los Angeles-Orange County coast. The Sierra and Rockies could see mountain snow. Over the weekend the trough moves into the Plains, where lee cyclogenesis brings storms and a Slight flash-flood risk Saturday from New Mexico and Texas into the central Plains. Models disagree on timing, with the GFS the outlier. Ahead of it, a central US ridge brings well above normal warmth through the weekend. AL93 should speed off northeastward late week. Southeast Florida has a Slight flash-flood risk Saturday, and a Gulf of Alaska trough reaches the Northwest. Ensemble means show a strong ridge off British Columbia, about +27 dam above normal at 6-10 days and still centered there at about +15 dam at 8-14 days. That points to a lasting western Canadian ridge, with troughs passing beneath it.

Climate Context

A very strong El Niño frames the season. The Oceanic Niño Index for July-September is +2.16, up from +1.80 the season before. It has risen every season since a cool -0.39 last winter, though the climb is slowing. Strong El Niño years favor an active subtropical jet and a busy eastern Pacific into late season, and both fit this week: Trudy's landfall in Mexico, EP94 now forming, and a western low pulling eastern Pacific moisture into the Southwest. In the Atlantic, the season's late activity is coming from a non-tropical coastal storm rather than tropical systems.
