Synoptic Picture

A semi-blocked ridge-over-East pattern begins its westward shuffle, keeping Northern Plains heat and Texas flooding running while the eastern trough starts to dig

The continent remains organized by the amplified, semi-blocked upper pattern that has dominated this week, but it is now on the move. At 500 mb a towering longwave ridge sits over the Ohio Valley and Mid-Atlantic with heights near 5900 dm, opposite a broad, nearly stationary trough draped from the Pacific Northwest into the Great Basin. That single ridge-trough couplet still explains most of the map: dangerous heat and humidity beneath the eastern ridge from the Northern Plains through the Upper Midwest and into the northern Mid-Atlantic (highs in the 90s to low 100s), and the water — monsoon storms and Texas flooding — living under and downstream of the western trough. The important nuance today is that the block is beginning to reconfigure. The medium range consensus retrogrades the ridge axis westward toward the Rockies and Plains while an eastern-U.S. trough digs in, a shift already previewed by the CPC 6-10 day outlook (above-normal West and Deep South, below-normal Great Lakes and Northeast). A cold front pushing into the Northeast today, with cooler air and Canadian wildfire smoke arriving in New England, is the leading edge of that adjustment.

The wettest, most dangerous focal point remains the southern tier, now in its third day. An upper-level low interacting with a deep Gulf moisture plume over a stalled Southern Plains boundary continues to train convection over the Texas Hill Country, where 6-8 inches with locally 10-plus inches are expected before the heavy-rain focus shifts toward Big Bend on Friday. This is fundamentally a flash-flood story rather than a severe-wind one. The Southwest monsoon is the other engine, with rounds of terrain-driven storms across the Four Corners and the greatest flash-flood threat near the Arizona-New Mexico border. The Canadian side deserves equal billing: a strong 1035 hPa Prairie high suppresses precipitation across the west, while eastern Canada is far more disturbed, with a Great Lakes/Ontario low and — on the 0000Z CMC chart — a deep sub-990 hPa cyclone near southern Manitoba anchoring the most active weather zone from the northern Plains into Ontario, all channeled by a blocking high over the North Atlantic and Hudson Bay.

The upper-level structure ties it together and points the way forward. The primary jet streak (50-70 kt) runs along the north and east flank of the ridge from the central Plains through the Great Lakes into New England, and that is exactly where today's modest severe threat lives, as a vigorous shortwave dropping out of Quebec drives low-topped, damaging-wind convection into northern New England. A closed upper low over the western trough supports the Montana hail/wind threat. The extended guidance keeps the large-scale wave train intact rather than collapsing: as a shortwave rounds the ridge into the Upper Midwest and Great Lakes early next week, the eastern trough deepens, the ridge slides west, and the dangerous heat eases across the northern states while consolidating and shifting south toward the central Plains, Mississippi Valley, and Gulf Coast. Note that GOES Air Mass RGB imagery was unavailable this cycle, so the airmass/PV boundary read leans on the 500 mb and surface analyses.

Beyond that, watch localized flash flooding near the Arizona-New Mexico border under the monsoon; isolated severe hail and wind overnight across northeast North Dakota and northern Minnesota as convection spreads south from the Canadian Prairies; and Canadian wildfire smoke fouling air quality across the Northeast behind the cold front.

Climate Context

This is textbook peak-summer North America: the North American Monsoon is in full swing over the Southwest, mid-summer heat and severe season is driving the heat domes and terrain convection, and the Atlantic hurricane season is beginning to stir. The tropical action is lopsided toward the East Pacific, which is the busy basin — Tropical Storm Elida southwest of Baja California, a disturbance south of Mexico given a high (90% through 7 days) chance of development by the weekend, and a lower-odds low southwest of Hawaii — while the Atlantic stays quieter, with only a 20% area forecast to form in the northeastern Gulf this weekend and drift toward Florida and the Southeast (WPC notes troughing there could enhance rainfall even without a true tropical low). The ENSO backdrop is a notable transition: the weak La Niña that held through early 2026 (ONI around -0.5) has been giving way to warming, with guidance trending toward a weak El Niño as the season progresses — a developing warm phase that will increasingly shape the second half of hurricane season.
