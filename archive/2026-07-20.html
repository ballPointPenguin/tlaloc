Three focal points organize the map. Aloft, the northern-tier jet remains the connective tissue. A 65-115 kt jet streak arcs west-northwest to east-southeast across the Upper Midwest and Great Lakes, placing its favorable left-exit region over the Ohio Valley and Mid-Atlantic — precisely the corridor into which tomorrow's severe threat expands. Full-disk imagery shows the jet looping well into the Canadian interior before recurving toward the Maritimes, keeping the whole regime slow and meandering. With the CPC 6-10 day outlook holding the ridge-trough couplet in place (90-100% above-normal confidence over the southern/central Rockies and Plains), the block is expected to persist rather than break down through late July.

Climate Context

This is textbook peak-summer North America. The North American Monsoon is in full swing over the Desert Southwest and Great Basin, with monsoonal moisture also interacting with the eastern Pacific over northwestern Mexico; mid-summer heat-and-severe season is driving both the northern-tier severe corridor and the building Plains-to-Lower-Mississippi heat dome. Both hurricane basins are active at or near their climatological ramp: the Atlantic is producing its first Gulf system in TD Two but is otherwise quiet with no additional formation expected over seven days, while the East Pacific remains the busier basin near its seasonal peak, running Tropical Storms Elida and Fausto plus a broad low well south of Mexico given a medium (60%) chance of development midweek. The ENSO backdrop is one of transition: guidance carries the state out of the weak La Niña that held through early 2026 and toward warm-neutral and eventually El Niño conditions, a warm shift that will increasingly shape the back half of the Atlantic season.
