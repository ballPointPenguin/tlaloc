Synoptic Picture

A maturing northern cyclone finally aims relief at the cornered southern heat dome — while the high-latitude ridge collapses and the West tips trough-dominant

The three-part meridional arrangement Tlaloc has tracked all week is still on the board, but the balance is tipping. The northern-stream system that ejected into Canada yesterday is now a mature 999-1000 mb low over the upper Great Lakes, trailing the day's tightest cold front southwestward through the central Plains and mid-Mississippi Valley — that corridor holds the strongest surface gradient and the most concentrated frontal lift. This is the front that will finally break the south-central heat late Wednesday. Aloft the pattern is a moderate-amplitude trough-ridge-trough: a broad longwave trough over the central U.S. with a weak closed low near Oklahoma/northern Texas, a ridge over the Intermountain West, and a second ridge over the Great Lakes and northeastern Canada. The southern Plains "cutoff" is worth a caveat — its innermost contour reads about 594 dam, a high value that marks a modest perturbation embedded in still-high heights rather than a deep, cold cutoff, so it does nothing to unseat the surface heat. Along the Gulf and Southeast a moist tropical airmass sits under a nearly isobar-free, slack gradient with a stalled boundary, leaving convection there to diurnal heating and low-level convergence.

Today's centers of action have quieted relative to yesterday. The severe threat has stepped down from yesterday's supercell/hail/tornado corridor to a wind-dominant SPC slight risk over central Iowa and far eastern Nebraska, where the cold front meets afternoon heating with 30-35 kt effective shear favoring line segments and damaging gusts; morning convection weakening and lingering inhibition cap the upside. Heavy rain is the co-threat on the front's cool side, with a slight excessive-rainfall risk in Iowa Wednesday and central Missouri into Thursday. The durable domestic signal remains the entrenched south-central heat — upper 90s to triple digits from the central Plains into the mid-Mississippi Valley through Wednesday with record warmth in play — before the front drops northern New England into the 30s Wednesday morning. In Canada, the interior British Columbia/Alberta ridge (a 1029 hPa center) sets up a firm gradient with a separate high to its south, while an active frontal system with 996-1000 mb lows works the BC/Pacific Northwest coast; the 988-992 hPa lows plotted far poleward are Arctic features, not a threat to the provinces. Over Mexico the North American Monsoon rolls on across the interior and Sierra Madre.

Aloft, the tightly packed contours and the sharp, cyclonically curved dry streamer curling from the upper Great Lakes into the Ohio Valley on the Air Mass RGB mark the jet corridor doing the work — that is the dynamical support behind the northern cyclone and its convection. The broad red-orange dry swath over the central and southern Plains is low-PV subsidence air under the late-summer ridge (its diffuse, warm-season character argues against a stratospheric intrusion), and midlevel warming, subsidence, and capping under it are why the heat holds, not any single surface high. The evolution question is whether the meridional regime is regenerating or genuinely transitioning, and the ensemble pair now leans toward transition. At 6-10 days the positive height anomaly sits over the Canadian Arctic while the Pacific Northwest and western Canada carry a well-below-normal trough and the ridge axis runs meridionally near 100-105W over the south-central U.S.; by 8-14 days that Arctic ridge has collapsed into a deep closed low, the positive anomalies have retreated to the southern U.S. and northern Mexico, and western Canada and the northern Rockies stay firmly trough-dominant. That is the ridge contracting geographically toward the southern tier — the classic retreat-toward-the-Southwest-while-the-north-cools signature — not the poleward regeneration read of yesterday. The WPC extended discussion supports this: a cold front sweeps the northern tier this weekend, a compact closed low drops into the Northwest and progresses into the northern Plains with moderate-to-heavy rain and northern Rockies wintry potential, another shortwave follows next Tuesday, and southern-tier ridging persists to keep the south-central heat and Four Corners monsoon going. Read together, the high-latitude/western amplification is fading toward a mobile, trough-dominant north and west, with the ridge surviving mainly as a heat anchor over the south.
