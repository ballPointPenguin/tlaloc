The southern tier stays warm (588 dam, above-normal) while the northern tier trends cooler and more active — the classic ridge-retreat-toward-the-Southwest signature rather than a wholesale breakdown, continuing yesterday's read.

This is peak-season North America running close to script for early September. The ONI table shows ENSO having warmed from slightly below normal (around -0.6 C) in early 2026 to roughly +1.4 C by summer, placing the basin in developing El Niño territory — and the tropics fit that state precisely: a crowded East Pacific with three hurricanes (Karina, Lowell, Marie) plus a 40 percent chance of another system well offshore southwestern Mexico, set against a suppressed Atlantic where, at the climatological hurricane-season peak, only Edouard's inland remnant carries interest and no development is expected for seven days. The North American Monsoon remains active, feeding daily Southwest and high-terrain convection. Severe weather is expressing itself in the expected late-summer mode — ridge-periphery, northern-stream and frontal storms across the northern Plains, Great Lakes, and Northeast rather than deep-Plains outbreaks — and the dominant domestic climate signal remains the durable central-U.S. heat. Teleconnections (weakly negative PNA, markedly negative NAO near -0.90 favoring Atlantic blocking, and a positive AO) are directionally consistent with fading western ridging and retained eastern/Gulf cutoffs, but as weak summer signals they serve only as a lagging cross-check on the height and PV fields, not as the basis for the story.
