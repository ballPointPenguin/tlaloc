Synoptic Picture

Isaias floods the Southeast, Rachel nears Southern California, and Category 4 Simon bears down on Mexico

Yesterday's three tropical storms are now coming ashore. Isaias landed near Destin as a hurricane. At NHC's final advisory it was a 30-kt post-tropical low near Montgomery, moving north at 14 mph. Rainfall of 4-8 inches, locally 15, is forecast for southern Alabama, the Florida Panhandle and Big Bend, and southwest Georgia. Elsewhere 2-5 inches are forecast, locally 10 in the southern Appalachians and coastal Carolinas, where WPC has a Moderate flood risk and landslides are possible. SPC's Slight Risk covers eastern Georgia into South Carolina along a warm front with mid-70s dewpoints and about 1500 J/kg of MLCAPE: brief tornadoes, peaking near the Georgia/South Carolina border from 21Z to 00Z. Rachel (50 kt, 992 mb) is racing east-northeast toward the Baja California/San Diego border tonight with 1-3 inches of rain, locally 5, and a Moderate flood risk near the Peninsular Ranges. Simon, a Category 4 with 130 kt winds and a 938 mb pressure, is 120 miles south-southwest of Manzanillo, still rapidly intensifying and due near west-central Mexico early Sunday. Up to 30 inches of rain is possible from Michoacán to Nayarit. Montana's first winter storm adds 10-20 inches of snow on the higher terrain.

Aloft, the southern two-thirds of the US sits under high, weakly curved heights of 583-584 dam, which is why the central US runs well above normal, with 90s in the southern Plains. The 12Z chart still shows a weak closed 580-dam circulation near the Florida Gulf coast. SPC expects that low to open up and lift through Kentucky and Tennessee into the Mid-Atlantic by Sunday night, with 45-55 kt southwest flow ahead of it. Out west, surface lows of 995-999 mb stretch from the northern Plains to the Great Basin, under a 55-70 kt jet streak and northern-stream energy dropping down the coast. That energy will pick up Rachel and carry its moisture inland, with cold air behind it feeding the Montana snow.

Sunday, a secondary low over Virginia brings a Slight Risk with a few tornadoes from central and eastern North Carolina into southeast Virginia. Rachel's remnant drives a Moderate flood risk over western Arizona, plus a brief tornado or two in the lower Colorado Valley. By Tuesday a positively tilted upper low over southern Nevada spreads flash flooding into the Great Basin and the first real snow to the Utah ranges. Ex-Simon moisture then fuels rain and possible severe storms from the southern Plains to the Mississippi Valley Tuesday-Thursday, while record heat (upper 80s to mid-90s) grips the southern Plains and Southeast. Models disagree on how fast the western low gets kicked east. The warm-West ridge regime has broken down: the 6-10 day positive anomaly (about 9 dam) is confined to the Southwest and northern Mexico, and by 8-14 days the core has moved to the central and eastern US.

Tropical Storm Warnings cover northwest Baja California. In Mexico, Simon's Hurricane Warning runs from Punta San Telmo to El Roblito, plus Las Islas Marías. Frost and freeze alerts are up in the Northwest. On Sunday, SPC expects scattered severe gusts in the Great Basin and central Rockies. High winds are possible in the Dakotas Thursday-Friday. No SPC mesoscale discussions are active yet.

Climate Context

This is a very strong El Niño: the Oceanic Niño Index reached +2.16 for July-September, up from -0.39 last winter, though each season's gain is now slightly smaller. The pattern fits the busy East Pacific: Simon and Rachel are active, NHC gives a new low south of Mexico a 70 percent chance of forming within 7 days, and the Atlantic is quiet for the week after Isaias. That a Pacific storm is reaching Southern California while a Mexican monsoon-type moisture plume feeds the interior West is unusual for mid-October. The CPC teleconnection indices are about 10 days old and not used here.
