Synoptic Picture

Bertha comes ashore under a Southwest PV cutoff while the week-long summer block reorganizes: heat ridge slides west, a wet regime builds across the East

The amplified, near-blocked summer regime Tlaloc has tracked for a week is not breaking down so much as rotating into a new configuration. The dominant 500 mb feature is a positively tilted longwave ridge over the Great Lakes and Ohio Valley (5880-5940 dm, 10-15 dm above normal), tied to a broad, flat subtropical ridge draped across the southern tier from the Desert Southwest to the western Atlantic and anchored at the surface by a formidable 1033 mb high over the central U.S. The freshest action, though, is at the ridge's southwestern flank: Tropical Storm Bertha is coming ashore near the Texas-Louisiana coast, and Air Mass RGB shows a vivid deep red-orange stratospheric PV intrusion — a cutoff over northern Mexico and western Texas — wrapping cyclonically into the storm's western quadrant. That upper-low/tropical interaction, distinct in the mid-levels from Bertha's surface center, is the organizing story over the south-central U.S. today.

Several centers of action define the map. The primary one is Bertha and its Southwest-cutoff partner: heavy tropical rain and coastal flooding along the northern Gulf, with the worst rainfall expected Friday as deep moisture pools against a quasi-stationary front over south Texas. Downstream, a slow-moving frontal boundary stalls from the Tennessee Valley through the Southeast and Mid-Atlantic, pooling warm tropical moisture and firing repeated convection with high rain rates — the eastern severe engine of earlier in the week has downshifted into a flash-flood regime. Organized severe potential is now modest and western: a Slight risk over western Kansas along the front and dryline, with more isolated damaging wind from the middle Texas coast into Deep South Texas (ahead of Bertha), the eastern Carolinas, and the Columbia Basin. Overlaying all of this is dangerous heat across the southern tier, heat indices of 105-115 F, set to expand north into the Great Basin and Northern Plains through the weekend, and the North American Monsoon firing daily storms with flash-flood risk over the Southwest and Great Basin.

Aloft, the pattern remains partially blocked, forcing systems to the extremes: the polar jet is suppressed well north across Canada — the full-disk imagery shows its sharp airmass boundary arching from the Canadian Rockies toward the Great Lakes — while the amplified interior ridge leaves the western CONUS in weakly anticyclonic, nearly stagnant flow with no meaningful shortwave energy. The Northeast sits under a downstream trough, the cool corner against the warm ridge. Canada carries real weight today: a deep 975 mb North Atlantic low trails a strong cold front through eastern Canada and the northeastern U.S., while a 993 mb Prairie low drives a moisture-convergence corridor across Saskatchewan and Manitoba. The WPC extended discussion argues this whole couplet persists and reloads through next week — the heat ridge warming and expanding west toward the Four Corners and south-central states, sandwiched between a West Coast trough and an increasingly unsettled, wet eastern trough that northern-stream energy will keep feeding. In short, the block reorganizes rather than resets.

Climate Context

This is textbook peak-summer North America. The North American Monsoon is in full swing over the Desert Southwest and central/southern Rockies, and mid-summer heat-and-severe season is driving both the persistent interior heat dome — the CPC 6-10 day outlook keeps a 90-100 percent above-normal signal over New Mexico, western Texas and the southern High Plains, with cool anomalies confined to the Great Lakes-to-Northeast trough — and the marginal severe and flash-flood threats along the fronts. Both hurricane basins are near their climatological ramp-up: the Atlantic is producing Tropical Storm Bertha at the northern Gulf Coast but is otherwise quiet with no additional development expected over seven days, while the busier East Pacific carries Hurricane Fausto well west-southwest of Baja and a trough south of southern Mexico given a medium (60 percent) 48-hour and high (90 percent) 7-day chance of developing, plus a low southwest of Hawaii likely to slide into the western Pacific. The ENSO backdrop is one of steady transition: after last year's weak La Niña (ONI near -0.5 C through late 2025), the index has warmed through neutral into weak El Niño territory by late spring 2026 (approaching +1.0 C in AMJ) — a warm shift that will increasingly shape the back half of the Atlantic season.
