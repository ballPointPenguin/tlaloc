Today’s Synoptic Picture

A locked-in summer block: Northeast heat ridge anchors a cutoff low over Texas, spinning up a multi-day flood threat while heat builds beneath the dome

Today's map is a textbook mid-summer blocking pattern, and everything worth watching radiates out from it. A strongly amplified longwave ridge (500 mb heights topping the 5800 dam contour) sits over the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic, while a broad, negatively tilted trough digs into the Pacific Northwest and northern Rockies. Wedged between them and pinched off from the westerlies is a cutoff low over central Texas and the Gulf Coast. Because the flow is blocked, nothing is moving — and in a stalled pattern the extremes amplify with each passing day: heat piles up under the ridge, and rain repeats over the same ground near the cutoff. That single story of persistence organizes the whole continent right now.

The centers of action sit at the edges of the block. The headline threat is the Texas Hill Country, where the cutoff low, a deep Gulf moisture feed under the Bermuda High, and a nearly stationary front are setting up a multi-day heavy-rain event with WPC advertising 6-10 inches over increasingly saturated soils and a rising flash-flood risk; SPC also flags a brief tornado threat in south-central Texas within the very moist, high-helicity environment. To the north, a dangerous heat dome grips the northern Plains, Great Lakes, and (spreading east) the northern Mid-Atlantic, with 90s to triple digits and WPC's extended discussion showing Major to isolated Extreme HeatRisk holding through the weekend before a shortwave finally nudges the ridge west early next week. A third focus is convection around the ridge periphery: an overnight round of severe storms is ongoing in northern New England, where SPC's Mesoscale Discussion 1623 tracks supercells across southern VT/NH under Severe Thunderstorm Watch 490, and a marginal severe risk persists in the smoke-tempered Mid-Atlantic. Finally, the North American Monsoon is firing scattered storms across the Four Corners and Intermountain West.

The upper-level structure explains why. The Air Mass RGB shows a vivid stratospheric dry intrusion — high-PV air descending — draped from western Kansas through Texas into northern Mexico, with the implied jet along its leading edge undercutting deep tropical moisture over the Gulf Coast. That undercutting configuration is exactly why the brightest deep convection is blossoming over east Texas and western Louisiana. Meanwhile a 105-115 kt jet streak runs along the southern flank of the Northeast ridge from the upper Midwest into the Atlantic; that strong mid/upper flow (60-80+ kt sampled by regional VWPs) is what is organizing the New England supercells despite modest instability. The key takeaway is that the block confers longevity: the cutoff has no steering, so Texas rain and southern-Plains convection recycle in place, while the ridge keeps the northern-tier heat entrenched. The WPC extended guidance keeps the ridge locked through the weekend before a shortwave dropping into the Great Lakes finally breaks the pattern early next week.
