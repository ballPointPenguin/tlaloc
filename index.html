This is peak-summer North America running close to the seasonal script. The North American Monsoon is supplying the moisture that WPC expects to spread north under the Southwest ridge for diurnal high-terrain convection, and severe-weather season is being expressed as ridge-periphery, northern-stream storms along a wavy frontal zone rather than classic deep-Plains supercells. That configuration typically favors the East Pacific while shear and dry air suppress the Atlantic, and today's tropics fit the split as the basin nears its early-September peak: the Atlantic is quiet with no development expected in seven days, while the East Pacific hosts Tropical Storm Lala west-southwest of Hawaii plus two disturbances (a 70 percent system south of Mexico and a 50 percent broad low southeast of Hawaii).
