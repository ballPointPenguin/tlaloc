The WPC extended discussion closes the loop for days 3-7: the durable upper high sets up over the Four Corners late week, monsoon moisture streams north under it to sustain south-central and Southwest heat, while an eastern trough deepens and, with improving model agreement, closes off an upper low over the Great Lakes by Sunday-Monday, easing Southeast heat. Climate Context

This is peak-summer North America running close to the seasonal script. The North American Monsoon is supplying the moisture WPC expects to stream north under the Southwest ridge for diurnal high-terrain convection, and severe-weather season is being expressed as ridge-periphery, northern-stream storms along a wavy Canadian frontal zone rather than classic deep-Plains supercells. The ENSO backdrop is a decisively developing warm state: the ONI has climbed out of last year's weak La Nina (about -0.6 in late 2025) through neutral in late winter to roughly +1.39 by MJJ 2026, a clear transition toward El Nino. That configuration typically favors the East Pacific while shear and dry air suppress the Atlantic, and today's tropics fit the split as the basin nears its early-September peak: the Atlantic is quiet with no development expected in seven days, while the East Pacific hosts Hurricane Lala near Hawaii plus a disturbance south of Mexico (80 percent in seven days) and a tropical wave west-southwest of Baja (60 percent). Teleconnection indices are only a weak, directional cross-check in summer: a strongly negative NAO (-1.28, -0.90 mean) that nominally favors Atlantic blocking and eastern troughing, an AO that swung positive and rolled back slightly negative, and a weakly negative PNA consistent with diminished western ridging — all corroborating, not driving, the deepening-eastern-trough and retreating-Southwest-ridge story the height and PV fields already tell.
