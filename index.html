Today’s Synoptic Picture

A blocked early-summer pattern pins the action over the Upper Midwest as a deep western trough faces off against a central Plains ridge

Today's map is a textbook battle of amplitudes. A high-amplitude, north-south longwave trough is dug into the Pacific Northwest, northern Rockies and Great Basin in a near-neutral to negatively tilted configuration, while a stout ridge (500 mb heights near 591-593 dm) anchors itself from the central Plains northeast toward the Great Lakes. That standoff makes the pattern more blocked than progressive and slow-moving, so wherever the energy is focused today, it is likely to linger. The single organizing story is that focus: a sprawling low pressure complex with multiple 993-1000 mb centers over the Upper Midwest and Great Lakes, sitting squarely beneath the diffluent exit region of a 60-80 knot jet streak rounding the base of the western trough. That favorable upper-level divergence is the engine driving the whole frontal system.

The centers of action radiate out from that low. First, the Upper Midwest and Great Lakes themselves, where the jet-exit forcing, surface cyclogenesis and convective towers already evident on satellite point to an active day of thunderstorms and heavy rain. Second, the trailing cold front, which sweeps southwest from the primary low through the central Plains and into Texas, tapping a rich Gulf moisture feed with dewpoints in the upper 60s to low 70s F across the lower Mississippi Valley and Gulf states. A narrow PV streamer dropping into Kansas and Oklahoma adds a focused source of mid-level forcing along that corridor, so the Plains-to-Texas front bears watching for organized convection. By contrast, the southeastern US is quiet for now: the 500 mb low near the Gulf Coast looks like a shallow warm-season thermal feature, and brownish-green Air Mass tones show drier mid-level air capping the region, holding convection to shallow cloud over Florida and the immediate coast.

The upper-level structure ties it all together and tells you why it matters going forward. The Air Mass RGB shows a pronounced stratospheric dry intrusion — high potential-vorticity air descending into the mid-troposphere — as a red-orange swath from the northern Plains through the central Rockies into the Desert Southwest, with the jet axis arcing from the central Rockies northeast toward the Great Lakes along the sharp boundary with deep green tropical moisture to the south and east. That PV-rich descent and the digging trough reinforce the western cold pool while the downstream ridge keeps the Southeast suppressed and warm. Because the block is slow to evolve, the moisture-rich frontal corridor from the Great Lakes back into Texas is likely to remain the repeated focus for lift and heavy rain over the next day or two. One caveat: the SPC Day 1 and Day 2 outlooks and the WPC short-range and extended discussions were all unavailable this morning, so this synthesis cannot attach official severe-weather categories or quantitative rainfall guidance — the convective signal here is inferred from the surface, upper-air and satellite fields alone.

Climate Context

The date places us in the heart of the spring-to-summer severe season, and today's setup — a sharp jet, ample Gulf moisture and a Plains warm sector — is a seasonally classic one even without the SPC products to grade it. The Atlantic hurricane season is underway (since June 1), but NHC expects no Atlantic, Caribbean or Gulf development over the next seven days; the tropical energy instead lives in the East Pacific, where Tropical Storm Cristina is spinning just south of El Salvador and a vigorous convective cluster with overshooting tops is firing over the Yucatan and Bay of Campeche. The Desert Southwest sits on the cusp of the North American Monsoon season, and the deep western trough and PV intrusion overhead are part of the pre-monsoon adjustment rather than monsoon moisture itself. One genuine gap: the ENSO dataset provided is mislabeled historical reference data from the 2002-2003 El Niño decay, not a current readout, so the present ENSO base state cannot be characterized from the material in hand and is best confirmed against the latest CPC ONI before drawing seasonal conclusions.
