Today’s Synoptic Picture

A blocked, high-amplitude pattern: heat ridge bakes the East while a deep Great Lakes low and Arthur's flooding remnants frame the action

Today's map is organized by one thing: a sharply blocked, non-progressive flow. A Rex/omega-style block has amplified an anomalous heat ridge over the central Appalachians and Mid-Atlantic (500 mb heights of 5880-5920 dm and a +10C-plus thermal anomaly over the Ohio Valley) while shunting a cutoff low into the Upper Great Lakes, where it has detached from the westerlies and a 991 mb surface cyclone is winding up. With the pattern locked, the weather is happening at the edges of the block rather than within it, and three distinct centers of action are stacked across the country.

The most dangerous of these is the Gulf Coast, where the remnants of Tropical Storm Arthur are wringing out torrential, training rainfall along the I-10 corridor from southern Mississippi to the western Florida Panhandle. WPC carries a High Risk of flash flooding with localized totals over six inches, and SPC notes that strengthening low-level winds in Arthur's circulation are supporting rotating cells and a tornado risk within the rainbands. The second focus is the Great Lakes-into-Northeast cyclone: a potent shortwave with a 90-kt mid-level speed max is driving a cold front through, and the combination of strong shear and backed surface winds favors low-topped supercells with damaging winds, a few tornadoes, and hail, while wind advisories and high-wind warnings fly across New York. A third corridor of damaging-wind storms runs down the Central Appalachians to the Mid-Atlantic as overnight convection redevelops behind the heating of the day. Meanwhile, beneath the ridge, south-central Texas roasts under extreme heat warnings with heat indices above 110F, and the southern Plains dryline near the 996 mb low offers a large-hail threat late.

The upper pattern is the key to both the threats and their longevity. The strong 70-90 kt jet streak curving cyclonically around the northern periphery of the ridge and cutoff low is the engine for the Northeast severe setup, and the Air Mass RGB confirms the dynamics nicely: a deep orange-red stratospheric dry intrusion blankets the central CONUS with the sharp PV/jet gradient running from the eastern Dakotas through Iowa and Missouri, right where overshooting tops are erupting near the Missouri-Arkansas border. A separate western PV streamer marks the digging Pacific Northwest trough feeding the block. Importantly, the block is poised to break: the extended guidance trends toward a faster ejection of the next wave, with troughing taking over the eastern two-thirds of the country this weekend into next week. That shifts the heavy-rain axis north into the Midwest and lower Great Lakes, brings broadly below-normal temperatures east, and builds the ridge westward instead, pushing Desert Southwest highs toward the 110s. So today is essentially the peak of the eastern heat ridge before the pattern relaxes.
