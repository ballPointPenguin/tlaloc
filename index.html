The upper structure ties it together. Climate Context

This is peak-summer North America running close to the seasonal script: the North American Monsoon is active, feeding high-terrain Southwest convection, while severe weather is expressed as ridge-periphery, northern-stream storms along a wavy frontal zone rather than classic deep-Plains supercells. The ENSO backdrop is a decisively developing warm state — the ONI has climbed from last year's weak La Nina (about -0.6 in late 2025) through neutral to roughly +1.39 by May-July 2026, a clear trend toward moderate El Nino. That configuration typically favors the eastern Pacific while shear and dry air suppress the Atlantic, and today's tropics fit the split as the basin approaches its early-September climatological peak: the East Pacific is busy, with Hurricane Lala near Laysan Island, Tropical Depression Two-C southeast of Hawaii, a high-odds (80%) system south of Baja and a moderate (50%) area south of southwestern Mexico, whereas the Atlantic shows only two low-end (20%) non-tropical or disorganized features near the Azores and east of Bermuda, with no organized wave or tropical cyclone evident on satellite.
