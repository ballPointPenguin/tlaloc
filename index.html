Today’s Synoptic Picture

A blocking eastern ridge locks in a dangerous heatwave while a ring-of-fire boundary keeps the storms confined to the north

Today's pattern is organized by a single dominant feature: a strongly amplified 500 mb ridge over the eastern third of the country, with 586-588 dam heights over the Ohio Valley and mid-Atlantic and an anchoring high over the western Atlantic. This is a semi-blocked, slow-moving regime, and it is driving a major-to-extreme heat event from the Midwest to the East Coast — widespread mid-90s to low-100s, heat indices of 100-115°F, warm overnight lows, and numerous possible record temperatures through the weekend. At the surface, a 1035 mb high over the central Rockies and a Bermuda-adjacent high offshore work together to suppress convection across the southern tier while pumping Gulf moisture (upper-60s to low-70s dewpoints) north into the Mississippi Valley. The out-of-place western trough over the Pacific Northwest and British Columbia is the only real progressive player, sending a shortwave through the Intermountain West into the central Plains.

The centers of action ride the ridge's northern periphery in classic ring-of-fire fashion. A sprawling 1008-1009 mb low complex over the northern Plains and upper Midwest anchors a quasi-stationary/cold-frontal boundary from the Dakotas through the Great Lakes, where the tightest surface gradient and best low-level shear reside. SPC carries an Enhanced risk over the Upper Midwest today, with supercells (large hail, severe gusts on ~40-45 kt deep-layer shear) growing upscale into bowing, wind-producing clusters this evening; deep convection is already showing on satellite near the Iowa-Wisconsin border. A second, moisture-driven threat sits over the southern Appalachians and Tennessee Valley, where extreme MLCAPE (4000+ J/kg) and high precipitable water favor wet microbursts. Excessive-rainfall/flash-flood concern overlaps the northern Plains-to-Great Lakes corridor. Down south, deep tropical moisture supports scattered diurnal storms along the Gulf Coast and Florida, while the Desert Southwest bakes under critical fire-weather conditions beneath the western high.

The upper-level context ties it together. The GOES-19 Air Mass RGB shows a pronounced stratospheric dry (high-PV) intrusion sweeping from the northern Plains through the Ohio and Tennessee Valleys, with a sharp boundary against the moist Gulf air marking the jet axis from the central Rockies toward the Great Lakes — the same axis where a 90-115 kt jet streak is analyzed along the ridge's northern flank. That jet and the descending PV air are exactly why the storm corridor is pinned to the north: forcing and shear live on the ridge periphery while the ridge core stays capped and blazing. Looking to Friday, the SPC Day 2 outlook expects the ridge to begin flattening and spreading west, keeping a Slight Risk from the Black Hills into the Midwest and lower Great Lakes and extending the threat into the Tennessee Valley and, notably, the Mid-Atlantic, where near-100°F heat and 60s dewpoints and efficient downdrafts favor damaging winds. In other words, the heat-plus-ring-of-fire regime persists into the weekend and nudges its severe threat eastward along the ridge's edge.
