Today’s Synoptic Picture

A blocked pattern splits the continent: cool, stormy West and northern tier against a building eastern heat dome

Today's pattern is organized by a single, stubborn idea: a blocked flow with a high-amplitude western trough and cutoff low parked over the Pacific Northwest, balanced against a sprawling, nearly stationary heat ridge over the central and eastern United States. That standoff does two things at once. It keeps the West 20-35 degrees below normal and stormy while critical fire weather persists over the Four Corners, and it pumps a summer heat dome into the eastern half, where heat indices of 100-110 degrees bring Moderate to Major HeatRisk. The boundary between these two regimes is where all the weather lives: a quasi-stationary front draped from the central High Plains through the Mid-Atlantic, riding the northern edge of the entrenched ridge.

The focal points cluster along that boundary. Heavy rain is the other half of the story: WPC hangs a Slight Risk for excessive rainfall over the Mid-Atlantic and a Marginal Risk over the Upper Midwest and northern Rockies/Plains as rounds of storms train along the slow-moving front. The SPC Day 2 outlook confirms the threat does not relent — a compact shortwave ejecting from the western trough keeps a Slight Risk over the central/northern Plains into the Upper Midwest on Monday, with a moist, high-CAPE airmass (4000+ J/kg, 50+ kt effective shear) over eastern North Dakota and northwest Minnesota, though persistent ridging keeps confidence modest on coverage.

Aloft, everything ties back to the 500 mb structure. The flat downstream ridge, with heights near 5920 dm over the Gulf Coast, is what caps the East under heat and steers disturbances along its northern periphery rather than through it. Because the pattern is blocked rather than progressive, this regime has staying power: the ridge holds the heat in place, and successive shortwaves rounding it will keep recycling the severe and flooding threat along the same northern-tier corridor for the next couple of days.

Climate Context

Seasonally this is peak severe and heavy-rain season for the northern Plains and Upper Midwest, and the setup — a persistent western trough feeding shortwaves into a warm, moist airmass on the north side of a heat ridge — is a textbook late-June regime. The broader climate backdrop is a fading weak La Niña: the Oceanic Niño Index sits near -0.5 degrees C but has been moderating, reaching about +0.48 degrees C by spring 2026 (MAM) and trending toward ENSO-neutral, leaning warm. With ENSO near neutral, it exerts little direct steering on the day-to-day pattern now. On the tropical front, the Atlantic and East Pacific hurricane seasons are both underway: NHC is watching a frontal low expected to form off the southeastern U.S. coast Monday-Tuesday (low, 20% through 7 days) and a tropical wave southwest of Baja California that could become a depression by midweek (medium, 50% through 7 days) before shear and cooler water undercut it. Neither poses an immediate threat, but both are reminders the season is ramping up. Meanwhile the persistent western trough leaves the Four Corners hot, dry and primed for critical fire weather even as the Southwest awaits the North American Monsoon.
