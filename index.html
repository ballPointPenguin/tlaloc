Today’s Synoptic Picture

The three-week summer block enters its endgame: the eastern cutoff opens and ejects while the fierce northern-Rockies ridge is set to retreat to its Southwest core

The high-amplitude block Tlaloc has tracked for three weeks is finally coming apart, and the eastern half has already changed character. Where yesterday's analysis found a fully cut-off cold-core low over the Mid-Atlantic, today's 500 mb analysis shows no closed low anywhere in the continental domain: the eastern trough has opened into a positively tilted, progressive feature sagging through the Mississippi Valley and Gulf Coast, and the associated surface low has slid offshore to a 1005 mb reflection off the Mid-Atlantic coast. The western anchor, by contrast, is still emphatically in place — a closed 5720 dm ridge over the northern Rockies and northern Plains with a core near 0 C, exceptionally intense for this latitude in late July, and the analysis notes it shows no sign of progressive translation. So today's snapshot is a half-broken block: passive, translating flow in the East; a blocked, near-stationary heat ridge in the West.

Centers of action ring the ridge and its downstream flow. The busiest is the northern rim, where a shortwave rounding the ridge's periphery is firing the day's severe threat: SPC carries a Slight Risk across South Dakota, Nebraska, and north-central Kansas, and MCD 1802 has supercells over central South Dakota and northern Nebraska with a watch likely (80 percent), the best deep-layer shear (20-30 kt) in an otherwise marginally sheared regime. A second, messier cluster runs along a front into eastern North Dakota and northern Minnesota (MCD 1803), and a third along the departing Southeast/Gulf boundary is outflow-dominant, high-CAPE/low-shear pulse convection (MCD 1801). Beneath the ridge, dangerous heat holds across the South with indices of 105-115 and is set to expand into the Southwest, Intermountain West, and northern High Plains this weekend, while the North American Monsoon keeps flash-flood and burn-scar concerns going over the Four Corners. Canada carries real weight today: a strikingly deep 979 hPa cyclone straddling the Ontario-Quebec border — unusually intense for midsummer — drives the tightest gradient on the continent into the Great Lakes corridor, while a Pacific front and 999 hPa low push onshore into British Columbia beneath a broad 1028 hPa western-Canada high.

Aloft the story is coherent. A high-PV streamer curling cyclonically into the Ohio Valley traces the leading edge of the now-open eastern trough. The regime question is whether the western ridge regenerates behind the disturbance now approaching southwestern Canada, and here the ensemble pair is decisive rather than ambiguous. Both the 6-10 day (Aug 4-8) and 8-14 day (Aug 6-12) height outlooks put the positive-anomaly core over the Desert Southwest and northern Mexico — not the northern Rockies — with western Canada, the northern Rockies, and northern Plains falling back to near-normal to only weakly above-normal departures beneath a deep, displaced Arctic trough over central Canada and more zonal-tending flow across the northern tier. WPC's extended discussion times the mechanism: a strong closed low into southwestern Canada Sunday-Monday drops a cold front that cools Montana by Sunday and the Dakotas by Monday, even as the ridge persists longest and hottest over the Southwest. This is a genuine geographic contraction, not a cosmetic flattening and not a wholesale collapse: the northern half of the block is transitioning out, the ridge survives by retreating to its climatological Southwest home. The strongly positive AO (7-day mean +0.95, up sharply) is a consistent cross-check, favoring suppressed high-latitude blocking and the more zonal northern-tier flow the height fields already imply; positive PNA still nods to a western ridge, but the ensembles are clear that the ridge's center of mass is moving south.
